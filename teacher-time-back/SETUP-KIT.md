# Safe AI Assistant Lab: School Setup Kit

## The pilot promise

Each participating teacher leaves with one assistant blueprint for one recurring task, a safe-input routine, a fictional test, and a human-review checklist. The pilot does not require student records and does not automate student-level decisions.

## Leadership setup, 45 minutes

Before inviting teachers, the school names:

- The approved AI tool and account type
- What information is allowed, uncertain, and prohibited
- Who answers privacy, legal, cybersecurity, special-education, labor, and procurement questions
- Which teacher tasks are permitted for the pilot
- How teachers report a concerning output or accidental disclosure
- Where assistant blueprints and approved templates will be stored

If these decisions are not available, run the workshop as a paper or fictional-material design lab. Do not ask teachers to improvise school policy.

## Task traffic light

### Green first builds

- Reuse approved lesson structures
- Create neutral directions and practice formats
- Draft schoolwide or classwide family updates from approved facts
- Prepare agendas and de-identified action lists
- Organize teacher-owned resources after records are removed

### Yellow, specialist or policy check first

- Translation and accessibility adaptations
- Differentiation for actual classes or groups
- Feedback frameworks and comment banks
- Internal documents with uncertain sharing rules
- Licensed curriculum content

### Red for this pilot

- Uploading identifiable student work or records
- Automated grading or student evaluation
- IEP, 504, behavior, discipline, eligibility, or placement decisions
- Health, counseling, immigration, family, or personnel records
- One-to-one student tutoring without explicit school guidance

## Teacher lab, 90 minutes

1. **Recognize the time drain, 10 minutes:** teachers identify one recurring task and estimate how often it happens.
2. **Choose the first build, 10 minutes:** screen for frequency, clear ownership, permitted inputs, and a visible output.
3. **Protect the inputs, 15 minutes:** confirm the approved environment and replace identifiers with fictional or neutral material.
4. **Build the blueprint, 25 minutes:** define role, sources, output, refusal rules, and human decisions.
5. **Practice, 20 minutes:** run one fictional test, identify assumptions and errors, then revise.
6. **Commit to review, 10 minutes:** check accuracy, bias, appropriateness, and alignment before any use.

## Each teacher leaves with

- One-task assistant brief
- Approved and prohibited input list
- Assistant instructions and refusal behavior
- Fictional test case
- Four-point review card
- Two-week measurement log

## Two-week measurement

Measure the workflow rather than celebrating output volume:

- Minutes spent on the task before and after
- Number of times the workflow was used
- Whether the draft was useful, partly useful, or discarded
- Number and type of corrections required
- Any unsafe-input attempt or concerning output
- Whether the teacher would keep, revise, or stop the workflow

Do not promise a fixed time saving. Use the pilot to learn which task, setup, and review routine actually help in this school.
