# Teacher Time Back Lab: Sales and Pilot Measurement Plan

Teacher Time Back should be improved from observed behavior and actual workflow reuse, not general enthusiasm about AI.

## Primary pilot outcome

# Minutes back

The primary outcome is **reported minutes recovered per teacher per week** from the recurring workflow the teacher actually tested.

Do not count only generation time. Include correction, review, troubleshooting, and any setup work required for the workflow to be usable.

A positive result means the workflow reduced total effort while remaining useful and reviewable. A workflow that creates more correction burden than value should be revised or stopped.

## Three measurement moments

### Before the lab

For one recurring task, record:

- What is the task?
- How often does it happen?
- How long does it normally take?
- What starting materials does the teacher usually have?
- What makes the task repetitive or difficult?
- What parts of the work must remain teacher judgment?

### During the lab

Record:

- Time spent using the assistant.
- Time spent correcting and reviewing.
- What the assistant handled well.
- What still required teacher judgment.
- Whether the output was usable, partly usable, or discarded.
- Any safety, boundary, or account confusion.

### 2–4 week follow-up

Record:

- Number of real reuses.
- Typical total minutes per use, including correction and review.
- Approximate minutes recovered in a typical week.
- Whether the teacher changed the saved instructions.
- Whether the workflow is still being used.
- Keep, revise, or stop.

## Secondary measures

### Reuse rate

Did teachers actually use the saved workflow again after the workshop?

### Teacher control

Could teachers identify what to change, reject, or stop when the assistant produced a weak result?

### Quality of starting output

Did the workflow produce a useful starting point without lowering instructional expectations?

### Safety and review

Did teachers stay inside the agreed data boundary, use the school-named account, keep missing information visible, and review outputs before use?

## Reporting rules

- Do not promise a fixed amount of time saved.
- Do not report gross time saved without subtracting correction and review time.
- Do not combine teacher estimates into a public claim without documenting the sample and method.
- Do not present a one-time successful prompt as evidence of a reusable workflow.
- Report sample size and follow-up period with any published result.
- Use medians when a small number of unusually large or small estimates would distort the average.

## Learning review after three pilots

- Identify the strongest first-task lane.
- Identify the most common approval, privacy, and platform confusion.
- Review whether the UDL/materials step improves the workflow or creates overload.
- Review whether the family-communication transfer example helps teachers understand portability.
- Compare in-person and virtual delivery friction.
- Review cohort size and timing.
- Review price against preparation, delivery, follow-up, and conversion.
- Decide which proof can be published with written permission.
- Revise the page, workshop, and outreach from repeated evidence only.
