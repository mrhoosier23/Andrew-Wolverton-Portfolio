# Sources and Claims Register

## Teacher time and guidance

- Gallup, June 24, 2025: "Three in 10 Teachers Use AI Weekly, Saving Six Weeks a Year." National web survey of 2,232 U.S. public K-12 teachers recruited from the RAND American Teacher Panel, March 18-April 11, 2025. Weekly users self-estimated 5.9 hours saved per week. Use with an explicit self-reported caveat. https://news.gallup.com/poll/691967/three-teachers-weekly-saving-six-weeks-year.aspx
- Gallup, May 2026: "Most Teachers Receive No Formal Guidance on AI Use." National survey of 2,069 public K-12 teachers. Only 18% reported receiving formal guidance from administrators on AI use. https://news.gallup.com/poll/710534/teachers-receive-no-formal-guidance.aspx

## NYC Public Schools

- NYC Public Schools, Guidance on Artificial Intelligence, 2026–27. Current guidance distinguishes restricted student-facing generative AI from teacher and staff use for instructional planning and operational tasks, subject to approved-tool, privacy, procurement, and professional-judgment requirements. Use the current policy page rather than the older vision-and-mission route. https://www.schools.nyc.gov/about-us/policies/guidance-on-artificial-intelligence
- NYC Public Schools, Artificial Intelligence Public Feedback Survey. Use for educator demand for clearer examples, educator resources, and AI-literacy support, with the stated respondent population and limitations. https://www.schools.nyc.gov/school-life/school-environment/artificial-intelligence-public-feedback-survey
- NYC Public Schools, Language Access Policy. Use for current language-access responsibilities, covered-language context, and the role of established school language-access processes. https://www.schools.nyc.gov/about-us/policies/language-access-policy

## Universal Design and instructional planning

- New York State Education Department, "Languages for All: Universal Design and Differentiation from the Start." Use to support the instructional-design rationale that learner variability is better considered during planning than only through later retrofitting. https://www.nysed.gov/world-languages/languages-all-universal-design-differentiation-start
- Handshake / Southern New Hampshire University, "Create a Universal Design Lesson Planner." Market-validation example for building reusable teacher-facing AI workflows around learner variability and lesson planning. https://joinhandshake.com/learn/create-a-universal-design-lesson-planner-133192fd/
- Handshake / Southern New Hampshire University, "Set Up a Teaching Deck Assistant." Market-validation example for carrying a lesson workflow into downstream teaching materials instead of starting from zero. https://joinhandshake.com/learn/set-up-a-teaching-deck-assistant-764bb4b3/
- Handshake / Southern New Hampshire University, "Reach Every Family in Their Language." Market-validation example for plain-language source preparation, protected-detail verification, draft translation, and human review. It is not authority for NYCPS language-access requirements. https://joinhandshake.com/learn/reach-every-family-in-their-language-55d88858/
- Handshake / Southern New Hampshire University, "AI for Teaching" collection. Use only as market validation that lesson planning, teaching materials, and community communication are being grouped as teacher-facing workflow categories. https://joinhandshake.com/learn/careers/ai-for-teaching/

## School guidance and privacy

- U.S. Department of Education Student Privacy Policy Office, "Personally Identifiable Information for Education Records." FERPA PII includes direct identifiers, indirect identifiers, and information that can identify a person through links with other information. https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/content/personally-identifiable-information-education-records
