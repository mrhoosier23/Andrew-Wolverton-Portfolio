# Leadership Setup Guide

## Safe AI Assistant Lab

Use this guide in the 45-minute setup conversation before the teacher lab. The goal is not to write a district AI policy. The goal is to create a clear, bounded environment in which teachers can practice one lower-risk use case.

## Decision 1: Name the approved environment

Record the exact tool, account type, and access route teachers may use.

If the school cannot name an approved environment, the workshop runs in paper/demo mode with fictional material. Teachers do not create accounts or test unapproved tools during the lab.

## Decision 2: Set the input lanes

### Green: use in the lab

- teacher-written instructions with no student information;
- published or school-approved curriculum excerpts the teacher may reuse;
- blank templates;
- fictional student-neutral examples;
- public dates, event details, or general logistics;
- de-identified notes only when leadership confirms the de-identification process.

### Yellow: pause and ask

- internal school documents;
- copyrighted materials without clear reuse rights;
- translations for high-stakes communication;
- personnel or operational notes;
- information that seems anonymous but contains a rare combination of details;
- any task touching grading, special education, discipline, safety, or legal obligations.

### Red: never enter in this workshop

- student names, initials, IDs, emails, addresses, birth dates, photos, voices, or biometrics;
- rosters, grades, attendance, behavior, health, disability, IEP, 504, counseling, or family records;
- identifiable student work;
- passwords, access tokens, or confidential staff information;
- any content leadership has not approved for the environment.

## Decision 3: Confirm the human decision line

The assistant may draft, organize, reformat, summarize neutral material, or propose options. It does not:

- grade or score a student;
- make placement, discipline, safety, disability, or eligibility decisions;
- send communications;
- replace required professional review;
- claim that an output is correct because AI produced it.

Teachers review accuracy, bias, appropriateness, and alignment before use.

## Decision 4: Select the cohort

- Recommended size: 8-20 educators.
- Prefer a shared planning context or grade band where possible.
- Invite one participating leader who can answer local policy questions.
- Do not use the session to evaluate teacher performance.

## Decision 5: Choose the first-build lanes

Select two or three lanes to emphasize:

- Reuse Planner: approved lessons, directions, unit structures, and checklists.
- Family Message Drafter: neutral logistics and general updates, never student-specific communication.
- Meeting-to-Action Assistant: neutral or properly de-identified agendas and notes.
- Teaching Library Organizer: teacher-owned, student-neutral resources.
- Feedback Framework Builder: rubrics and neutral feedback stems, never student scoring.

## Day-of readiness check

- [ ] The approved environment and account are named on the opening slide.
- [ ] Teachers can sign in before the lab begins.
- [ ] The school's use and privacy guidance is available.
- [ ] The escalation contact is named.
- [ ] The fictional practice pack is distributed.
- [ ] Teachers understand that no student records are needed.
- [ ] A leader will participate or remain reachable.

## Follow-up data

The two-week learning summary should include only cohort-level counts:

- number of teachers who tested an assistant;
- number choosing keep, revise, or stop;
- median self-reported minutes before and after, if enough teachers report;
- common correction categories;
- safety slips or unclear policy questions;
- next support needed.

Do not use the workshop data for teacher evaluation.
