# Facilitator Guide

## Safe AI Assistant Lab: 90 minutes

### Facilitation stance

Lead with workload, not AI features. Keep the entry point small. Demonstrate a finished assistant before asking participants to build. Make every safety rule visible in the artifact itself. Never imply that speed is more important than professional judgment.

## Materials

- Teacher Workbook
- Completed Reuse Planner Example
- Fictional Practice Pack
- Safe Input and Review Cards
- school-approved tool and account instructions
- timer and display
- optional printed copies for paper/demo mode

## 0-10 minutes: Name the time drain

Opening promise: "You are not here to master AI. You are here to stop rebuilding one piece of work."

Show the completed Reuse Planner. Point to five things:

1. one task;
2. one approved source lane;
3. a never-use boundary;
4. a predictable output;
5. a teacher decision.

Prompt participants to write one sentence: "I keep rebuilding..."

## 10-25 minutes: Protect the inputs

Name the approved environment. Explain that tool approval and task approval are separate questions. Use the green, yellow, and red cards.

Run the identifier scan:

- Who is named?
- What number, date, place, image, voice, or combination could point to a person?
- Does the task require student information at all?
- Can a blank, public, fictional, or teacher-owned source do the job instead?

Checkpoint: every participant lists approved inputs and never-enter inputs.

## 25-40 minutes: Choose and bound the task

A strong first task is frequent, repetitive, easy to verify, and useful with student-neutral sources. It does not make a regulated or student-level decision.

Use the four filters:

- Repeatable: does it happen often?
- Neutral: can it work without student records?
- Reviewable: can the teacher quickly tell whether the output is usable?
- Bounded: can the job be described in one sentence?

If any answer is no, narrow the task or choose another lane.

## 40-65 minutes: Build the blueprint

Participants complete six fields:

- Role: what job does the assistant perform?
- Sources: what may it use?
- Output: what exact structure should it return?
- Quality: what makes the draft useful?
- Refusal: what must it stop and flag?
- Human decision: what stays with the teacher?

Use the copy-ready instruction frame in the workbook. Encourage plain language. Avoid prompt jargon.

## 65-80 minutes: Test and review

Use only the Fictional Practice Pack. Teachers run one test or rehearse the test in paper/demo mode.

Review in order:

1. Accuracy: what is true, assumed, missing, or invented?
2. Bias: whose context or perspective is treated as the default?
3. Appropriateness: is the tone, level, and format suitable?
4. Alignment: does this support the teacher's actual goal and local rules?

Revise the blueprint once. The goal is not a perfect output. The goal is a reliable correction loop.

## 80-90 minutes: Save and measure

Teachers save:

- assistant name;
- copy-ready instructions;
- one fictional test;
- review checklist;
- two-week measurement log.

Close with: "The assistant drafts. You decide. If it takes longer to correct than to do yourself, revise it or stop using it."

## Two-week follow-up: 30 minutes

- 0-5: anonymous pulse and wins.
- 5-15: compare before/after minutes and correction work.
- 15-23: diagnose common failure patterns.
- 23-28: each teacher chooses keep, revise, or stop.
- 28-30: name unanswered policy or support needs for leadership.

## Common moments and responses

### "I do not know anything about AI."

"That is fine. You need one task and your professional judgment. The structure does the rest."

### "Can I paste a student's work if I remove the name?"

"Not in this workshop. Names are only one kind of identifier, and school rules may be stricter. Use the fictional practice pack and ask your privacy lead about the real workflow."

### "Can it grade for me?"

"It can help build a rubric or neutral feedback framework. The student-level evaluation and final score stay with you."

### The approved tool is unavailable

Switch to paper/demo mode. Complete the blueprint and rehearsal. Do not create a workaround account.
