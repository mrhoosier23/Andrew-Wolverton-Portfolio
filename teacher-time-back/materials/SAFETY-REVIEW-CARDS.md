# Printable Safety and Review Cards

## Card 1: Before anything goes in

### Check the environment

- Is this the exact school-approved tool and account?
- Does local guidance permit this task?
- If unsure, stop and ask the named school contact.

### Scan for identifiers

Look for names, initials, IDs, emails, addresses, dates of birth, photos, voices, grades, attendance, behavior, health, disability information, family records, and combinations of details that could identify a student.

### Use a safer source

Prefer a blank template, public information, fictional example, teacher-written source, or approved student-neutral material.

## Card 2: Green, yellow, red

### Green: proceed in the approved environment

Blank templates, fictional examples, public facts, teacher-owned directions, and approved student-neutral curriculum material.

### Yellow: pause and check

Internal documents, translations for high-stakes communication, de-identified records, personnel notes, regulated decisions, or any rare combination of details.

### Red: never enter in this workshop

Student records, direct or indirect identifiers, identifiable student work, grades, rosters, behavior, health, disability, counseling, family, or confidential staff information.

## Card 3: Review every output

- Accuracy: What is true, assumed, missing, or invented?
- Bias: Whose context or perspective is treated as the default?
- Appropriateness: Is the tone, level, accessibility, and format right?
- Alignment: Does it support the task, local guidance, and my professional goal?

Decision: use after editing, revise the assistant, or stop.

## Card 4: The human decision line

The assistant may draft, organize, reformat, summarize neutral material, and offer options.

The teacher keeps instructional judgment, student-level evaluation, grading, communication approval, accommodations, safety decisions, and final use.

The assistant drafts. People decide.
