# Teacher Time Back Lab: Comprehension Test Kit

Use this kit before sending outreach. It tests whether a person can understand the offer without Andrew explaining it for them.

## Who to test

- Two K-12 teachers who use little or no AI.
- One school leader who helps decide professional learning, technology, or instruction.
- Do not use a close collaborator who already knows the project.

## What to send

Send only the public page: `https://www.awolverton.com/ai-schools.html`

Do not send the workbook, facilitator playbook, or an explanation first. Ask the reviewer to share their screen or think aloud while they explore.

## Teacher test: 12 minutes

Say: “Please look at this as if a colleague sent it to you. I will not explain it yet. Tell me what you think is happening as you go.”

After five minutes, ask:

1. What problem is this workshop trying to solve?
2. What would you personally do during the 90 minutes?
3. What would you leave with?
4. What do you need to bring or prepare?
5. Where does student information fit, if anywhere?
6. Who checks and decides whether the result is usable?
7. Show me the finished example.
8. Show me the safety practice.
9. What still feels vague, technical, risky, or tiring?
10. On a scale from 1 to 5, how confident are you that you could participate without prior AI experience?

Pass criteria:

- Both teachers identify “time back” as the main benefit.
- Both can describe the build as one recurring task, five plain-language decisions, one practice result, teacher review, and a two-week test.
- Both know they bring a laptop and one recurring task, not student work or a prepared prompt.
- Both understand that the school names the tool and account.
- Both can find and operate the example, lab walkthrough, and quiz without coaching.
- Neither expects automated grading, a compliance certification, or guaranteed time savings.
- Average confidence is at least 4 of 5.

## School leader test: 12 minutes

After five minutes of independent review, ask:

1. What is being purchased?
2. Who is the pilot for, and how many people participate?
3. What happens before, during, and after the teacher session?
4. What does Andrew provide?
5. What must the school decide or provide?
6. What is explicitly outside the pilot?
7. What evidence would tell you whether to continue after two weeks?
8. What would you need to confirm before approving the pilot?

Pass criteria:

- The leader accurately states the price, cohort size, setup, lab, and follow-up.
- The leader can name the school’s responsibilities: tool and staff account, local guidance, escalation contact, cohort, and laptops.
- The leader understands that Andrew teaches a build and review method and does not certify privacy, legal compliance, or platform approval.
- The leader can explain the keep, revise, or stop measurement decision.

## Decision rule

Do not send outreach if any reviewer cannot explain the product, believes real student information is required, misses what teachers leave with, or cannot operate the interactive walkthroughs. Revise the confusing point and retest only that point with a new reviewer.
