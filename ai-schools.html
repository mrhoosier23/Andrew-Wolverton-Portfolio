Why Andrew

Built from a teacher's point of view.

Andrew taught high-school theatre and English, then moved into arts management, marketing, development, production, and technology-enabled workflow design. The lab combines those experiences: practical instruction, clear boundaries, live facilitation, and systems people can actually reuse.

The goal is not to turn teachers into AI specialists. It is to help them recognize one repetitive task, build a bounded assistant around it, and keep professional judgment visible.

Practical questions

What schools usually want to know first.

Is this an AI literacy training?

No. The lab is narrower. Teachers build one reusable assistant for one recurring task. Concepts are introduced only when they help teachers make that build safer or more useful.

Do teachers need to bring student data?

No. The flagship lab uses Andrew's practice materials. Teachers are explicitly told not to use real student records in the session.

Which AI platform does the lab use?

The school chooses the approved tool and staff account during the setup call. The build method is designed to work across mainstream AI assistants rather than requiring a specific vendor.

What if our district has stricter rules?

The school's rules govern the session. If a use case, file type, or workflow is not clearly allowed, the lab pauses instead of improvising around the policy.

How do we know if the assistant is worth keeping?

Teachers run a two-week test and consider both time saved and correction work. The outcome can be keep, revise, or stop. A polished output does not automatically count as success.
